# Market Entry Strategy Guide

Reference guide for solo founder market entry decisions. Load on demand during Positioning and Landscape modes.

---

## 1. Entry Decision Matrix

Cross-reference market stage with buyer type to find your recommended entry pattern.

| Market Stage → | Emerging | Growing | Mature | Declining |
|----------------|----------|---------|--------|-----------|
| **B2C** | Experience Simplify | Niche Down | Pricing Disrupt | Don't enter |
| **B2B SMB** | Platform Wedge | Niche Down | Pricing Disrupt | Niche Down (dying giant's niche) |
| **B2B Mid** | Workflow Integration | Platform Wedge | Experience Simplify | Don't enter |
| **B2B Enterprise** | Don't enter (solo) | Don't enter (solo) | Don't enter (solo) | Don't enter |
| **Prosumer** | Experience Simplify | Niche Down | Experience Simplify | Pricing Disrupt |
| **Developer** | Platform Wedge | Workflow Integration | Niche Down | Don't enter |

**"Don't enter" means**: This combination is structurally hostile to solo founders. The sales cycle, compliance requirements, or capital needs are prohibitive.

## 2. Five Entry Patterns — Detailed Playbook

### Pattern 1: Niche Down

**Core idea**: Serve one specific segment so well that broad competitors can't match you.

**Step-by-step**:
1. Identify the segment: Find user complaints about broad competitors. Look for "[tool] for [profession]" searches.
2. Talk to 10 people in the niche. Ask: "What's the most annoying thing about [current tool]?"
3. Build ONLY what the niche needs. Ignore everything else.
4. Use niche-specific language on landing page — they should feel "this is built for ME."
5. Go where they gather: specific subreddits, Slack groups, conferences, newsletters.

**Validation signals** (first 30 days):
- People in the niche share it with peers without being asked
- Users say "finally, something for [niche]"
- Conversion rate from niche channels > 5%

**Expansion path**: Adjacent niches with similar workflows → broader market.

### Pattern 2: Platform Wedge

**Core idea**: Build the best extension/plugin/integration on an existing platform.

**Step-by-step**:
1. Pick a platform with: (a) large user base, (b) app store/marketplace, (c) visible gaps.
2. Find the top-requested features the platform hasn't built. Check their feature request forums.
3. Build it. List it. The platform provides distribution.
4. Optimize for platform marketplace SEO (title, description, screenshots).
5. Get reviews fast — first 10 reviews determine your marketplace ranking.

**Validation signals** (first 30 days):
- Organic installs from marketplace without marketing
- Platform features your app or links to it
- Users request features that expand your scope

**Expansion path**: Multi-platform support → standalone product.

**Risk**: Platform builds your feature. Mitigate by going deeper than the platform will.

### Pattern 3: Workflow Integration

**Core idea**: Don't replace tools. Connect them. Fill the gaps between them.

**Step-by-step**:
1. Map the workflow: What tools does your target user use daily?
2. Find the manual gap: Where do they copy-paste, re-enter data, or switch contexts?
3. Build the bridge. Automate the hand-off between tools.
4. Demo the "before/after" — show time saved in seconds.
5. Distribution: launch on directories (Product Hunt, etc.), target "I hate [manual step]" searchers.

**Validation signals** (first 30 days):
- Users describe you as "the glue between [X] and [Y]"
- Daily active usage (workflow tools need to be habitual)
- Integration with more tools is the #1 feature request

**Expansion path**: More integrations → workflow automation platform.

### Pattern 4: Pricing Disrupt

**Core idea**: Offer 80% of the leader's value at 20% of the price.

**Step-by-step**:
1. Identify the leader's pricing page. Find the tier gap — the jump from free to paid, or from $X to $XXX.
2. Build for the users stuck in that gap. They need more than free, can't afford enterprise.
3. Price at the "no-brainer" point: low enough that there's no approval process needed.
4. SEO: target "[leader] alternatives", "[leader] pricing", "[leader] too expensive."
5. Comparison page on your site: honest feature comparison showing what you don't have AND what you do.

**Validation signals** (first 30 days):
- Users say "I switched from [leader] because of price"
- Organic traffic on "[leader] alternative" keywords
- Low churn (users are happy with 80%, don't miss the 20%)

**Expansion path**: Add premium tier, expand features, move upmarket gradually.

### Pattern 5: Experience Simplify

**Core idea**: Take something complex and make it ridiculously simple.

**Step-by-step**:
1. Find the product category where users say "I wish [tool] was simpler."
2. Identify the 20% of features that serve 80% of users.
3. Build ONLY those. Make the first-use experience take < 60 seconds.
4. Create a "wow" demo: record yourself doing the complex thing in 30 seconds.
5. Distribution: Twitter/X thread showing the demo, Product Hunt launch, blog post.

**Validation signals** (first 30 days):
- Users share your product unprompted ("look how easy this is")
- Time-to-value < 2 minutes (users get immediate result)
- Users describe you as "the simple [category tool]"

**Expansion path**: Add features carefully (don't become what you disrupted).

## 4. When to Pivot Your Entry Strategy

Signals that your current approach isn't working (after 60 days):

| Signal | Meaning | Action |
|--------|---------|--------|
| Zero organic signups | Wrong channel or wrong audience | Test different audience or channel, not product |
| Signups but no retention | Product doesn't solve real pain | Do customer interviews, find real pain point |
| Users ask for different features | You're in right market, wrong positioning | Reposition around what users actually want |
| Competitor launched same thing | Your edge isn't defensible | Niche down further or pivot differentiation axis |
| Can't explain in one sentence | Positioning is unclear | Rewrite positioning canvas from scratch |

**Pivot protocol**:
1. Talk to 5 users who left + 5 who stayed
2. Identify the pattern
3. Adjust ONE thing (audience, channel, or positioning — not all three)
4. Give it 30 more days
5. If still failing after 2 pivots → the market may not be right for solo entry

## 5. Competitive Response Playbook

What to do when competitors make moves against you.

### When a competitor copies your feature
- **Don't**: Panic, publicly complain, rush to add more features.
- **Do**: Double down on your differentiation axis. They copied the feature, not your positioning.
- **If they copy everything**: Go deeper into your niche. They can't serve your specific audience as well as you.

### When a competitor cuts price below yours
- **Don't**: Match their price (race to bottom).
- **Do**: Emphasize value, not price. Add messaging about what makes you worth the premium. If you can't justify premium → add a free tier to capture price-sensitive users.

### When a competitor gets funded
- **Don't**: Try to out-spend or out-hire them.
- **Do**: Move faster. Funded companies add process and slow down. Ship the features they'll take 6 months to ship.
- **Prepare for**: Their marketing spend to increase. Your organic/community channels become more valuable.

### When a competitor acquires a company in your space
- **Don't**: Assume they'll integrate perfectly (acquisitions usually slow both companies down).
- **Do**: Talk to users of the acquired product — they're scared of changes and open to alternatives.

### When a competitor shuts down
- **Do**: Create a migration guide. Target their users with "switching from [dead competitor]" content.
- **Do**: Reach out directly to their users if possible (forums, social media).
- **Don't**: Celebrate publicly (bad look). Just quietly capture their market.
